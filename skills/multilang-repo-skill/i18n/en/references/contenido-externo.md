# New content with external data (C1–C9)

Read it with [contenido]: when the work incorporates claims about the outside world (versions and
compatibilities, platform limits, official names, standards, dates, figures, clinical or legal data).

**C1 Form before volume.** By default, an index of sources (what each thing is, who publishes it, where,
its status and when to refer to it), not the reproduction of third-party texts. Reproduce only if the
person asks and the license allows it. If you reject a datum for lack of a source, the index is the
alternative you offer. If without a network you can only name sources from memory, name them in the answer
and in pending items as `no-comprobado` (unchecked), with the domain that is needed (C8), so that the
person checks them; they do not enter the repository's index until checked (R10).

**C2 Primary source first.** The publication of the body or author that issues the datum, the
manufacturer's official documentation or the official register. A search result or a summary is secondary;
if you can, open the primary source.

**C3 Source level in the content itself.** `oficial` (official), `segunda-mano` (second-hand) or
`no-comprobado` (unchecked), with the entry rule of R10. Distinguish `no-aplica` (not applicable: the
datum does not exist in that language or context) from `no-comprobado` (it may exist, but you have not
found it).

**C4 Validity.** `vigente` (in force), `sustituido` (superseded), `retirado` (withdrawn), `anulado`
(annulled), `obsoleto` (obsolete) or `no comprobado` (unchecked), with the date consulted. Never claim
validity without confirming it. A withdrawn datum stays as withdrawn, with the indication not to use it as
in force.

**C5 Validation of the content.** Mandatory fields, closed values for level and validity, a mandatory
source for every datum with text, unique identifiers and a reference to the index from the instructions.
At scale S a checklist is enough; at M and L, a script with its sabotage (P7).

**C6 Discrepancies between sources.** Set them out with the weight of each. Do not present as an open
doubt what a single isolated source holds against several concordant ones, nor the reverse. Spreading
confidence equally among unequal sources is a false balance.

**C7 An automatic finding is not a fact.** Before changing a datum because a bot, another model or a
person without a source says so, check the primary source. If you cannot, lower the claim to what you do
know and leave a record.

**C8 What is unchecked goes to pending items** (R10), with the missing source and the necessary network
domain. With [alto-riesgo], a specialist reviews the content before any use, and the output itself says
so.

**C9 Rigor proportional to risk.** For a low-risk datum (the minimum version of a tool), the official
documentation and the date consulted are enough. A high-risk datum (doses, legal deadlines, safety limits)
requires an official primary source, no datum from memory, the date consulted and specialist review. Never
invent data or references: every reference must be retrievable (title, issuer, URL or identifier, date).
Remove citation marks that do not refer to an identifiable source and express the uncertainty in words
instead of hiding it.
